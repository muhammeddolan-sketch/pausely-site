Privacy isn't a feature here. It's the architecture.

Most health apps promise not to misuse your data. Pausely is built so that there's nothing to misuse — because your data never reaches us in the first place.

No account

Nothing to sign up for. No email, no phone number, no identity.

No backend

There is no Pausely server holding your logs, because there is no Pausely server.

No trackers

No analytics SDK, no ad network, no third-party libraries phoning home.

Deleting is deleting

Remove the app and the data is gone. No "request deletion" form, no 30-day retention.

Questions

Is it really free?

Logging is free and unlimited, along with the last 7 days of history. Premium ($6.99/month, $39.99/year, or $59.99 once) unlocks the Trends screen, unlimited history and PDF reports. During the beta, Premium is free.

If there's no cloud, how do I move to a new phone?

Export an encrypted backup from Settings, move the file however you like, and restore it on the new phone with your passphrase. The file is useless to anyone who doesn't have that passphrase — including us.

What happens if I lose my backup passphrase?

The backup cannot be opened. There is no recovery, because a recovery mechanism would mean we hold a key — and we don't. Keep the passphrase somewhere safe, like a password manager.

Are the insights medical advice?

No. They're plain statistics on your own logs: the app compares your average severity on days with a tag against days without it, and only speaks up when the gap is large enough and there's enough data. Correlation isn't cause, and the app says so on every card.

What does the app read from Apple Health / Health Connect?

Sleep, and only if you turn the switch on in Settings. Read-only — Pausely never writes to either store, never saves what it reads, and never includes it in backups. Turning the switch off stops all reads.

Is Pausely a medical device?

No. It doesn't diagnose, treat, or give medical advice. It's a notebook that does arithmetic. Talk to a healthcare professional about your symptoms.
